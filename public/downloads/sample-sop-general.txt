Word limit: 800–1,000 words (adjust per program requirement)

========================================
PARAGRAPH 1 — OPENING / HOOK (100–120 words)
========================================
[Start with a compelling story, moment, or observation that led you
to this field. Avoid clichés like "Since I was a child..."]

Example opening:
"When I spent three months working with rural farmers in [Country] as
part of a development project, I saw firsthand how [specific problem]
affected [specific outcome]. That experience crystallized my conviction
that [your field] holds the key to addressing [the broader challenge].
It is this belief — and the urgency I feel — that now drives my
application to the [Program Name] at [University]."

========================================
PARAGRAPH 2 — ACADEMIC BACKGROUND (150–180 words)
========================================
[Describe your undergraduate/previous education, key courses,
academic achievements, and how they prepared you for this program.]

"My academic foundation was built at [University], where I studied
[Field] and graduated [with distinction / ranking]. During my studies,
I was particularly drawn to [specific area], which I explored in my
thesis on [topic]. Under the supervision of Prof. [Name], I [what you
did and what you found]. This experience taught me [specific skill or
insight] and confirmed my commitment to [field]."

========================================
PARAGRAPH 3 — RESEARCH / PROFESSIONAL EXPERIENCE (200 words)
========================================
[Describe your most relevant experience — research, work, or projects.
Be specific. Show evidence of capability, not just interest.]

"Following graduation, I joined [Organization] as [role], where I
[specific achievement or project]. One highlight was [describe a
concrete project/outcome in 2–3 sentences]. In parallel, I [another
experience — publication, conference, award]. These experiences have
equipped me with [specific skills] and deepened my understanding of
[specific theme]."

========================================
PARAGRAPH 4 — WHY THIS PROGRAM / UNIVERSITY (150 words)
========================================
[Be specific — mention faculty, labs, courses, research centers.
Show you have done your research. Never use generic praise.]

"I am applying specifically to [University] because of [Faculty
Member]'s work on [specific topic], which aligns directly with my
research interest in [your topic]. I am also drawn to the [specific
lab / center / research group] and the interdisciplinary approach of
the [Program Name]. The opportunity to [specific thing the program
offers — fieldwork, collaboration, funding] is particularly compelling."

========================================
PARAGRAPH 5 — YOUR GOALS (100–120 words)
========================================
[Short-term and long-term goals. Be realistic and connect them to
your background and the program.]

"Upon completing this program, I intend to [short-term goal, e.g.,
pursue a PhD / return to my country to work in X sector / join X
type of organization]. In the long term, I aim to [broader ambition
that connects to your home country or global impact]. This program
is the clearest pathway toward those goals."

========================================
PARAGRAPH 6 — CLOSING (60–80 words)
========================================
"I am confident that my academic preparation, research experience,
and commitment to [field] make me a strong candidate for this program.
I am excited by the prospect of contributing to [University]'s
scholarly community and I look forward to the opportunity to grow
as a researcher and scholar. Thank you for considering my application."

========================================
CHECKLIST BEFORE SUBMITTING YOUR SOP:
========================================
□ No spelling or grammar errors
□ Addresses the specific program and university by name
□ Shows evidence, not just claims ("I am hardworking" → show it)
□ Connects past experience to future goals
□ Within word/page limit
□ Does not repeat the CV — adds new narrative
□ Reviewed by at least one mentor or advisor
